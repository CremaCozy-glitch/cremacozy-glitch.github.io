My shots started tasting off a couple of months into owning my Barista Express. Not terrible. Just a little stale, like the coffee had lost its edge. I figured it was the beans, swapped them out, and nothing changed.

It wasn't the beans. It was old coffee oil sitting in the group head.

Backflushing is the fix. And honestly, it's one of those jobs that sounds scarier than it is. You're basically pushing water back through the machine to flush out the gunk. That's it.

Here's how I do it on my own machine, when I do it, and the small stuff I got wrong the first few times.

## What backflushing actually does

A backflush pushes water backward through the group head and the three-way valve. Sounds fancy. In practice, it just flushes out the coffee oils that build up after every shot.

Those oils go rancid. Slowly, quietly, and you don't notice until one day your espresso tastes bitter for no clear reason. That's the oil talking, not your beans.

One thing to be clear on: backflushing and descaling are not the same job. Backflushing handles coffee residue. Descaling handles the mineral scale from your water. If your machine needs a descale, my [Breville Barista Express descaling guide]({{ '/how-to-descale-breville-barista-express/' | relative_url }}) is the one you want instead.

## What you need

Not much. The portafilter, the 1 CUP basket, the grey cleaning disc that came with your machine, and fresh water. That's the water-only setup.

For the deeper clean, add one cleaning tablet. I use the <a href="https://link.amazon/B074ZpzR7-v" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" target="_blank">Breville cleaning tablets</a> since they're made for the machine's cleaning cycle. Any compatible tablet works, but I've stuck with these.

## How to backflush with water only

This is the quick one I run most weeks. It takes a few minutes and keeps things from getting bad in the first place.

This is the quick version I do regularly between full cleaning cycles.

1. Fill the water tank.
2. Drop the 1 CUP basket into the portafilter.
3. Set the grey cleaning disc inside the basket.
4. Lock the portafilter into the group head.
5. Press the 1 CUP button and let it run for around 10 to 20 seconds.
6. Stop the shot, then wait a few seconds.
7. Pull the portafilter out carefully and dump the water.
8. Do that 3 to 5 times.

It's not a deep clean. But it clears out the loose residue before it turns into something worse.

## How to backflush with a cleaning tablet

When the CLEAN light is flashing, or when it's just been a while, this is the full version.

1. Fill the water tank completely.
2. Put the 1 CUP basket into the portafilter.
3. Add the cleaning disc, then sit one tablet on top.
4. Lock the portafilter into the group head.
5. Place a large container under both the group head and the steam wand.
6. Make sure the machine is off.
7. Hold down **1 CUP** and **2 CUP**, then press **POWER** while still holding the other two.
8. Keep all three pressed for about 10 seconds. The cycle starts.
9. Let the machine run it all the way through.
10. Wait for the two beeps at the end.
11. Remove the portafilter, discard the tablet residue, and rinse everything well.

## How often should you backflush?

Here's the schedule I actually stick to.

- **Water backflush:** once a week
- **Cleaning tablet backflush:** when the CLEAN light flashes, or roughly every 2 to 3 months

If you're pulling several shots a day and steaming milk on top of that, do the water flush more often. It costs you two minutes and keeps the oils from piling up.

## Signs your machine wants one

You don't have to guess. The machine usually tells you, one way or another.

- Espresso starts tasting bitter or stale
- The group head looks grimy from underneath
- Extraction gets messier than usual
- Water flow feels uneven
- The CLEAN light starts blinking

If the grinder is the thing acting weird, that's a different problem entirely. I go through that in my [Barista Express grinder troubleshooting guide]({{ '/barista-express-grinder-not-working/' | relative_url }}).

## Mistakes I made (so you don't have to)

**Using the wrong basket.** You want the 1 CUP basket with the cleaning disc. Not a normal brew basket full of coffee.

**Thinking a backflush fixes scale.** It doesn't. Different problem, different fix.

**Forgetting the drip tray.** A backflush pushes a lot of water through. If the tray is half full already, it overflows. Ask me how I know.

**Skipping the rinse after a tablet clean.** If any tablet residue is left, run a short water flush before you make a drink. Otherwise your first coffee tastes like cleaner. Not a good surprise.

## Do you need a tablet every time?

No. For regular upkeep, a water-only backflush does plenty on its own. The tablet is for the deeper cycle, when residue has built up or the machine asks for it.

I think of them as two levels. Quick weekly flushes, and the full tablet cycle when it's actually needed. Keeps things simple.

## FAQ

**Is backflushing the same as the CLEAN cycle?**  
Close, but not quite. People use the words interchangeably, and I get why. The CLEAN cycle is the machine's guided deep-clean. A water-only backflush is just a manual rinse.

**Can I backflush every day?**  
You can do a water flush that often without hurting anything. But you don't need to. Once a week is fine unless you're using the machine heavily.

**Do I need to remove the shower screen?**  
Not for a normal backflush. That's a separate, deeper cleaning job.

**Why does the first shot taste weird after cleaning?**  
Leftover cleaner or stale water in the system. I always pour the first one out after a tablet clean.

**What if the CLEAN light stays on?**  
That usually means the cycle didn't finish, or the machine wants something else. I go into that in my guide on [why the CLEAN light won't turn off]({{ '/breville-barista-express-clean-me-light-wont-turn-off-fix/' | relative_url }}).
